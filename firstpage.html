My Instructional Design Philosophy

To be effective, learning should be purposeful, relevant and centred on the learner. Learning should go beyond simply providing information; it should create opportunities for learners to connect new knowledge to their experiences, practise what they have learned and apply it in meaningful situations.

My approach to instructional design begins with understanding the learner and identifying the actual learning or performance need. The learning experience should be designed with a clear purpose, appropriate activities and opportunities for application and feedback. Technology can enhance this process, but its use should be guided by the learning need rather than the technology itself.

Good instructional design should result in learning that is not only understood but can be transferred into improved performance.

Principles That Guide My Approach

  1. Start with the need - understand the problem before determining the learning solution.
  2. Know the learner - consider learners' experiences, needs and existing knowledge.
  3. Make learning relevant - connect learning to situations learners are likely to encounter.
  4. Encourage application - give learners opportunities to practise and apply what they learn.
  5. Evaluate and improve - use feedback and results to strengthen future learning experiences.

Theories That Shape My Practice

Constructivisim

Learning Through Experience

Learners build knowledge by connecting new information with their existing experiences. In the workplace, this can be supported through realistic scenarios, problem-solving, discussion and opportunities to apply learning on the job.

Adult Learning

Learning With Purpose

Adult learners bring experience to the learning environment and are more engaged when learning is relevant, practical and connected to real needs. This aligns well with workplace learning, where employees need to see how learning relates to their roles and performance.
